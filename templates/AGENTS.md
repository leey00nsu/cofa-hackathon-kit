## Cofa hackathon workflow

The official task README and current exam instructions take priority. Preserve provided files and existing instructions. Unless assigned a bounded worker/reviewer role, act as the main agent and the participant's only conversation partner. At startup the main reads `.hackathon/INTERVIEW_PROTOCOL.md`. Workers read their assignment and relevant evidence/guides without restarting the customer interview or taking over shared records. Before implementation read `.hackathon/AUTOMATED_TESTING.md`; consult its command reference only when needed. Do not load every skill or reference at startup.

### Environment and timing

- Use the provided Codex and permitted models; participant guidance names 6.1 Sol, 5.6 Terra and 6.0 Luna. Check actual platform labels rather than inventing model IDs. Do not update Codex, connect external AI/personal keys/subscriptions, or change platform/proctor settings. Use tokens and resources for the task.
- Public instructions, skills, templates and partial code may be imported within the exam rules. Do not import private repositories, personal PC files or a complete external solution. Used repositories must remain public until second-round results. The participant uses platform AI for external information rather than browsing outside directly.
- Do not push Git, create external issues/PRs, deploy outside, or transmit task files, answers or logs. Public material imports do not authorize outgoing transfers. Do not bypass restrictions; report platform failures through Cofa Helper or the provided issue channel.
- Computer Use/browser automation is prohibited, including Playwright/Puppeteer/Selenium UI tests. Use CLI, unit tests and permitted local HTTP requests; the participant operates Browser manually. Bind the app to `0.0.0.0`, report its actual port/path, and keep its server running. Do not assume port 3000 or require a tunnel.
- Use the current exam notice and actual timer for the date, implementation deadline, question count and response time. Do not reuse the earlier exam's schedule for an additional exam. Allow for question-generation waits within the absolute deadline; ask the participant for remaining time when necessary. Personal rehearsals use their stated conditions.
- If an extension notice and timer disagree, have the participant clarify through the official channel; until confirmed, preserve work and submission time against the earlier deadline. For platform/proctor failures, prepare only observed time, stage, action, error, prior recovery/result and remaining time for the participant to report; redact sensitive details. Do not assume an extension, recovery or saved submission.
- Reserve time for final regression, participant UI checks, implementation Q&A, review and direct submission; reduce scope when late. Check required files/save state and leave submission time. Finish AI-assisted study before submission is fixed, then stop AI use. Do not answer live follow-up questions or assume notes/code remain accessible; follow-up answers are typed by the participant without AI or copy/paste.
- Before that AI-stop point, give the participant a short checklist distinguishing file save, submission confirmation, any officially required follow-up and final completion display. Use only stages that actually exist and report only observed stages. After confirmation the participant completes and checks the remaining process independently; do not automate submission or solicit live prohibited questions.

### Customer discovery and Matt skills

The participant relays questions and real customer answers. Business facts come from those answers or the official task; technical facts come from local inspection. Prioritize uncertainties that change behavior, acceptance criteria or scope. Follow the interview protocol's six discovery areas and implementation entry criteria; never generalize one example into unstated rules. When official material already defines the relevant flow and criteria, record that evidence and skip redundant questions. Reuse existing answers and propose customer workflow feedback after the first working slice and before scope freeze. Proposed questions are not confirmation.

Distinguish confirmed customer outcomes/constraints, explicitly delegated developer choices, and unanswered business facts. For “that is the developer's job,” identify the actual question's scope and retained constraints; link the real Q to D, choose within that scope, record the actual decision-maker/reason/alternatives/limits, and implement without asking the same approval again. Keep customer goals/constraints in R and self-chosen policies in D, labeled in acceptance criteria where relevant. Delegation confirms the boundary, not the chosen policy or a test result; it alone does not make work Blocked. Do not expand a technical deferral into permission to choose unrelated business rules or override existing requirements. If the boundary or required business outcome is unclear, ask about concrete results/constraints rather than technical methods and hold only dependent work. With no official question limit, impose no total-question/round cap; batch size and feedback checkpoints are not caps. Customer agreement with a described flow never replaces actual execution/UI evidence.

Use only needed skills and keep Matt's original SKILL.md unchanged. If invocation is unavailable, read the bundled skill and apply its procedure with available tools; do not update Codex or add plugins/MCP/accounts. If permitted independent reviewers are unavailable, accurately label a same-session review rather than claiming independent review.

### Resume, failures and permitted delegation

At a real session change, use Matt's handoff procedure and its OS temporary-directory location; keep references to existing project records instead of copying them. Add actual HEAD/working changes, related R/check IDs, unresolved customer questions, server command/port/owning session, and one next action. Mark unknown outcomes as unknown. Do not force session resets or repeat approvals during normal progress. If the temporary location is unavailable under platform rules, preserve a minimal redacted note in the existing ignored study area and report that fallback.

Pass the exact handoff file path, target workspace path and role/scope together. On resume match them to the current request; never substitute the newest or similarly named handoff when the correct location is missing. Preserve the pointer to any pending answers-hidden rehearsal in the existing study record. Missing context blocks only dependent work, not independent confirmed work.

On resume, read the existing handoff and project records, inspect current files/Git changes with `review-scope` when available, and use `audit-requirements` to compare recorded evidence. Reuse only applicable current checks; do not restart the project, duplicate running work/servers, or rerun everything merely because the session changed. A pending or interrupted command is not a successful result: inspect its process, outputs and owning session before retrying. Distinguish app failures, tool/permission failures and pending participant/customer input. When the same failure repeats without new evidence, change the approach or reduce scope within the remaining time, preserve the error and next action, and continue independent work without bypassing restrictions.

The main should proactively delegate independent, bounded work when it materially helps and is supported/permitted; this project requests such delegation without another approval for each assignment. Before assigning or accepting parallel work, read `.hackathon/references/orchestration.md`. Prefer actual native subagent tools, otherwise use permitted provided CLI executions managed by the main, otherwise continue in the main and report the actual limitation. Do not require the participant to manage worker sessions. Keep small/dependent critical-path work in the main and continue meaningful independent work while workers run.

Assign R/Q/D references, grounded acceptance criteria, exact workspace/basis, disjoint product/test files, interfaces, unresolved versus delegated choices, time bounds and a result route. Each worker owns its TDD slice. One writer owns each file; the main owns customer originals, shared R/D/V, GLOSSARY/ADR/DEFENSE, common configuration, integration server and same-checkout Git operations. A worker's original Matt implement commit step yields to this ownership; a separate worktree commit needs explicit assignment. Give reviewers read-only scope; workers do not spawn their own agents or message the participant/customer directly. These are workflow rules, not technical file locks or sandbox guarantees.

Route worker questions back through the main: inspect existing answers first, decide technical/explicitly delegated choices within constraints and record important D, and relay only new customer-dependent questions to the participant without waiting for every worker to finish. Pause only dependent work. After a real answer, update the same Q/R/criteria/D and explicitly send the changed scope to affected workers; confirm receipt/application rather than assuming shared files refresh active contexts. Track actual native session/process IDs and current assignment/result pointers briefly in existing local handoff/work notes or ignored `.hackathon/study/parallel.md`. CLI question-pending runs may return and resume with an updated task; do not silently start a second writer or select an ambiguous last session.

Workers return basis/changes, real checks/logs, decision proposals, questions and limits. The main compares current instructions/answers, reads their files and evidence, integrates on stable sources, and obtains applicable integration checks before marking R Verified. Inspect late/uncertain tasks before replacement and keep prior failures/results. Before final study/submission, collect pending results and confirm owned writers/runs have stopped or returned; do not leave AI workers running past the AI-stop point or aid actual prohibited Q&A.

The main agent or one assigned owner coordinates shared dependency installation, common runtime/config changes and integration-server restarts. Different source files do not make changes to the same `.venv`/`node_modules` independent. Workers return the need and affected work/checks; communicate the actual outcome before reusing affected evidence. Actually isolated environments can proceed independently. This adds no install lock, mandatory installation or approval for every test.

### Requirements and evidence

Reuse `.hackathon/REQUIREMENTS.md`, `.hackathon/DECISIONS.md` and `.hackathon/VERIFICATION.md`; do not add a parallel PRD/ticket/evidence system. The installed manifest's `requirements_path` identifies the existing requirements source of truth. Preserve IDs and update only changed entries, briefly.

Before the first implementation, briefly summarize the problem/benefit, users/current workflow, main input-to-completion flow, first slice and R IDs, final mandatory versus first-slice/deferred scope, and important unknowns/constraints in that requirements source. Reuse an equivalent existing section; add it in place only if missing, preserving existing content. This is the lightweight PRD role, not another file, approval, version series or completion gate for every field. Detailed rules/acceptance/status remain in R rows; link Q/D/V rather than copy them. Keep certainty (confirmed/assumed/unknown), scope (included/deferred/excluded) and the existing progress states distinct. A first-slice MVP never removes official final requirements.

Use the existing implementation-readiness section for a short ready / questions-needed / partial-ready decision with related R IDs, evidence, consequential unknowns and the next action. Understand the overall goal and main flows, but resolve detailed rules only as needed for the selected slice. Start when its input/output, start/end and successful/error behavior are grounded; continue independent confirmed work when a business rule blocks another part. Preserve TDD: R/acceptance → failing behavior test at an agreed seam → minimal real implementation → actual verification. Do not request another approval for each slice.

Before implementing or assigning a grounded flow, the main briefly explains its logic strategy in that same readiness section: related R/evidence and example input/result, processing/branch order, core data structure/algorithm and interfaces, grounded error/state behavior, important reasons/actual alternatives/tradeoffs via D, and verification seam/cases/independent expected values. Reuse existing equivalent records, mark planned files as planned, and distinguish estimated resource costs from measurements. Keep it to a few lines, update only affected parts after new evidence, and send changes to workers. This is not another design document, approval or requirement to settle every detail before work.

- After each relayed answer, separate facts from hypotheses, assign confirmed requirements R IDs, link actual Q/answer or official task locations, and define observable acceptance criteria. Propose important unanswered questions; proceed with independent confirmed work. Do not turn a convenience request into customer-confirmed P0.
- For data tasks, inspect provided files read-only and connect the row meaning, identifiers/join relationships, units/time, duplicates/missing values and calculation population to the related R/Q. Distinguish observed file facts from customer-confirmed meaning; never silently choose a denominator, normalize identifiers or drop repeats. Select relevant numeric/ML checks from AUTOMATED_TESTING only when the task calls for them; do not add a parallel data ledger.
- Before implementation identify the related R IDs. Record important decisions with their reasons, alternatives and actual files. Do not portray retrospective reasoning as historical intent or planned files as existing implementation.
- If new customer/official evidence, contradictions, required exceptions or actual constraints change meaning/scope, update the same summary and affected R rows while preserving IDs/history. A behavior or acceptance change must not exist only in the summary: execution bindings compare the R fields, not prose meaning. Reassess affected code/checks and distinguish confirmed business changes, technical choices in D, and unresolved business questions. Preserve current valid checks for unaffected work; final mandatory items remain incomplete until actually satisfied.
- For important D entries, connect AI proposal, actual decision-maker/adoption or change, the reason actually stated, and the observed outcome/check. Capture real participant correction events as observation → request → kept/changed/rejected → recheck. Do not attribute autonomous AI work to the participant, invent missing judgment/failure stories, or request approval for every technical detail.
- Status meanings: Proposed = unconfirmed; Confirmed = customer/official evidence, including an explicitly delegated boundary but not customer endorsement of our D choice; Implemented = grounded required behavior implemented with verification outstanding; Verified = actual evidence for every acceptance criterion; Blocked = a recorded dependency prevents progress. Partial implementation stays Confirmed with the remainder noted. Preserve prior state/evidence when blocked and continue independent work. Reuse these states; developer discretion is recorded in Q/D, not a new progress state.
- Link each R to real procedures, commands, inputs, expected/observed results, pass/fail and execution IDs. Actual result cells use `check:<id>` or `manual:<id>`. Never claim an unrun test passed, fabricate missing questions/results, or promote partial coverage to Verified. Reassess after relevant code, criteria, answer or environment changes; retain past results as history.
- Check full execution-log preservation along with command results. New command records include the log's content digest; missing/altered logs fail the audit. Historical records without that digest remain unchanged and explicitly unverified for this added check, not retroactively upgraded. Do not describe audit/QA output with `unverified_log_ids` as complete evidence verification. Manual participant attestations remain distinct and do not require command logs.
- An answer-like heading cannot override an explicitly pending/empty answer. Requirements outside a valid table header are format errors, not silently omitted work. Q&A also reports stale or unknown applicability of referenced executions/observations; keep historical results while correcting the explanation and obtaining only necessary current evidence.
- Keep raw relayed Q&A in Git-ignored `.hackathon/private/INTERVIEWS.md`; use `## Q1` with `질문:` and `답변:` for actual conversations. Keep only minimal summaries and Q references in the ledger. If the original question was not preserved, say so. Never import hidden personas/evaluation files or credentials.
- Logs and study outputs stay in ignored `.hackathon/checks/` and `.hackathon/study/`; installed `docs/DEFENSE.md` is also local and ignored by default. Use only publishable minimal customer summaries/Q references there, never raw private material. Ignore rules do not protect already tracked files or erase Git history. Check tracked files/history separately; summaries, code and synthetic data also require publication review. Never publish without the user's separate approval; exam transfer restrictions still apply.

Markdown records, skill invocation and structural audit PASS do not guarantee official scores, evaluator access, semantic correctness or participant understanding.

### Documents the participant can understand

Write participant-facing requirements, decisions, verification summaries, DEFENSE, Q&A, review sheets and handoffs in plain Korean a non-developer can understand. Explain who does what, how the program handles it, the visible result, and why that choice helps before presenting technical evidence. Use short sentences and concrete inputs/results already supported by the project. Avoid unexplained acronyms; define necessary terms on first use. Put exact paths/functions/commands/check IDs after the explanation as confirmation evidence, retaining them for reproducibility. Separate customer confirmation, our design choices, actual observations and unknowns in everyday words. Do not simplify away exceptions, failed checks or limits, and do not alter raw logs/code/IDs to make prose easier. In rehearsals use readable questions and judge understanding of behavior/reasons rather than jargon recall; reveal explanations only after the participant answers.

### Implementation, verification and completion

Own intermediate verification using the automated testing guide: task-specific tests at real production boundaries, isolated data/test-owned servers, execution, failure analysis, fix and rerun of the original procedure under a new ID. Do not substitute a duplicated app/calculation or weaken customer criteria to pass tests. Preserve participant data and the running app.

Do not request a click test for every feature. Keep business-rule and textual workflow questions active. Automatic PASS with final UI pending remains Implemented where UI is an acceptance criterion and does not block independent work. After automatic regression, collect a final UI checklist in VERIFICATION with actual R IDs, URLs, inputs/actions and expected observations. Record only relayed real observations; after a UI fix rerun affected automatic checks and relevant UI steps.

Use `check --requirement <R-ID>` for real execution records. Zero tests/all skipped or todo are NOT_RUN; expected-failure or unexpected-pass-only runs are executed but UNVERIFIED, not ordinary successes. Unrecognized summaries or changing sources are not verified. Before completion use `audit-requirements` and read actual files for review; consult `.hackathon/references/validation-tools.md` for command syntax and limitations. Select the guide's optimization/comparison, input-to-result freshness and blank-preview checks only when the actual task needs them.

After each small user flow is implemented and automatically checked, update only its short entry in `docs/DEFENSE.md`: input-to-output flow, actual paths/functions, business rules, implemented exceptions, self-chosen policies/reasons/rejected alternatives, real commands/results/check IDs, unverified conditions and limits. Reuse R/D/V/ADR records instead of copying ledgers. Distinguish customer/official confirmation from our choices and unanswered assumptions; propose consequential business unknowns to the customer. Mark missing historical reasons/alternatives as unrecorded. Keep Matt's domain-modeling and ADR rules; do not refactor or interrupt development to fill documentation. Recheck affected explanations after code/rule/evidence changes, including failures.

After the first working flow and consequential rule/logic changes, explicitly review missing counterexamples as part of the existing code-review Spec axis. Use a supported/permitted read-only reviewer when helpful, otherwise label the main's own review accurately. Inspect actual R/Q/D, code and tests for relevant boundary/state/order combinations. Return concrete candidate inputs/actions, expected behavior and its source (or an unanswered business question), current test coverage and observed versus unexecuted status. The main relays unresolved business policy, assigns actual defects to the current owner for failing-test/fix/recheck, and records remaining risk in existing V/R. Do not invent requirements or claim a proposed case passed; use the testing guide and avoid an extra full review for each small edit.

Do not leave participant understanding entirely to a final request. After the first automatically checked core flow (even with final UI pending) and important decision changes, the main proactively gives a short plain-Korean explanation of the actual input-to-result flow, customer rules versus our choices, recorded tradeoffs and verification limits, then offers one unaided prediction or explanation on a different grounded input/choice. Do not invent a tradeoff or historical reason when none was recorded. Put it in the next useful dialogue update, not every small edit, and continue independent development while an answer is pending or deferred. Preserve actual prompt/response/feedback/current R/D/code references in ignored `.hackathon/study/DEFENSE_REHEARSALS.md`, without a new score or ledger; missing responses remain pending. Before explaining, check for an active answers-hidden rehearsal: do not reveal its unanswered material or silently switch modes. After an actual response, compare current code/evidence, use targeted supplemental learning if needed and recheck affected understanding after changes. Honor participant deferral, current exam/AI-stop rules and actual unaided-answer limits.

Before submission is fixed, invoke `$implementation-brief` to produce `.hackathon/study/IMPLEMENTATION_QA.md` and `LAST_REVIEW.md`, using current DEFENSE entries as an index and checking actual code/evidence. Reuse final checks that are still applicable; a repeated study request alone does not require new executions. Run `python3 .hackathon/tools/kit.py verify-qa --workspace .`; stale/unknown evidence is marked and returns 2 even when documents match. Leave participant review time. Recheck Q&A after source/evidence changes. Invite the participant to explain key choices when available; unanswered invitations do not prove understanding or stop independent work. The participant confirms submission manually.

When the participant says “Q&A 리허설 시작”, follow `.agents/skills/implementation-brief/references/defense-rehearsal.md` instead of showing study answers: inspect current implementation/requirements/publishable evidence/Git/tests and DEFENSE, present exactly five implementation-specific questions first, then wait for unaided answers. Do not show answers/hints or open LAST_REVIEW for the participant first. Preserve questions, question-time references/fingerprint and pending numbers in ignored `.hackathon/study/DEFENSE_REHEARSALS.md` before waiting; append only actually received participant responses and feedback. Resume that mode without revealing answers. Compare question-time and current evidence first; defer affected questions after relevant changes rather than grading an old premise as wrong. Assess eligible answers for accuracy, omissions and wrong assumptions, then give learning locations. New rounds also hide answers until the participant responds. Practice output is not proof of understanding or an official score. Do not run this mode or assist answers during an actual AI-prohibited Q&A or a submission-locked AI-stop period.

After an answer reveals a specific misunderstanding, use only that flow for supplemental learning: trace the same real input and its value changes through 3–5 actual file/function locations, labeling executed observations versus code interpretation and unknowns. Reuse existing checks unless new observation is needed. Then ask for an unaided explanation/prediction on a different input and wait before assessing it. Keep answers in the ignored rehearsal record; never expose unanswered rehearsal answers early or refactor the product merely to explain it.

Report four things briefly: implemented requirements; actually verified requirements; unconfirmed/unimplemented items; known failure conditions.
